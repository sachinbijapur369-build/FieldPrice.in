Tonnage Calculation Reference

How much tonnage do you need? A quick reference.

Required tonnage depends on material type, thickness, bend length and die opening. This table gives approximate tonnage per metre for common materials — multiply by your bend length in metres.

MaterialThicknessApprox. Tonnage per MetreTypical Die Opening (V)
Mild Steel1.0 mm8–10 t/m6× thickness (6 mm)
Mild Steel2.0 mm20–25 t/m8× thickness (16 mm)
Mild Steel3.0 mm35–42 t/m8× thickness (24 mm)
Mild Steel6.0 mm100–120 t/m8× thickness (48 mm)
Mild Steel10.0 mm250–300 t/m8× thickness (80 mm)
Stainless Steel2.0 mm30–38 t/m8× thickness (16 mm)
Aluminium2.0 mm12–15 t/m8× thickness (16 mm)

Tonnage rule of thumb

For mild steel air bending: Tonnage ≈ (1.2 × S² × L) / V, where S = thickness in mm, L = bend length in m, V = die opening in mm. Stainless steel requires ~1.5× more tonnage than mild steel for the same thickness. Always add 20% margin for tooling variations.

Buyer's Checklist

Know these six things before buying a press brake.

Press brakes are high-value capital equipment with a 15–20 year life. Getting the specification right now prevents expensive upgrades or limitations later.

  • Maximum material thickness — Know the thickest material you will bend, including future growth. This determines minimum tonnage.
  • Maximum bend length — Your longest part determines the bed length. Standard sizes: 2500, 3100, 4100 and 6100 mm.
  • Production volume — High volume justifies CNC with 3D programming and automated backgauge. Low volume may only need NC or basic CNC.
  • Tooling requirements — Standard V-dies, radius tools, gooseneck punches, hemming tools — clarify what is included in the quote and what is extra.
  • Controller level — Match the controller to your complexity. 2D is sufficient for simple bends; 3D programming pays for itself in complex multi-bend parts.
  • Safety & service — Laser safety systems are increasingly mandatory. Verify authorised service, spare parts lead time and training scope at your location.
