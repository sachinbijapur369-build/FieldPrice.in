How to Read a Belt Marking

Decode what each part of a belt code means.

Every belt has a code printed on it. Understanding these markings helps you order the correct replacement without errors. Here are the most common formats.

SPA 1250 → SPA = wedge section (17mm top width), 1250 = effective (datum) length in millimetres. This is the most common industrial format.
B68 → B = classical section (17mm top width), 68 = inside length in inches. Older machines often use this format. B68 ≈ SPA 1750.
8PK 2120 → 8 = number of ribs, PK = micro-V / ribbed profile, 2120 = effective length in mm. Common in automotive and HVAC applications.
HTD 8M-1120-30 → HTD = curvilinear tooth profile, 8M = 8mm pitch, 1120 = belt length in mm, 30 = belt width in mm. Precision timing drives.
XPZ 1060 → XPZ = cogged/moulded wedge section (same width as SPZ, but with teeth for better flexibility), 1060 = effective length. Better heat dissipation on small pulleys.

Buyer Notes

Belt drive buying — what you should know.

Belts are consumable but critical. Wrong length, wrong section, or unmatched sets can cause machine damage and unplanned downtime.

Can you source matched belt sets? Yes. We verify matched sets are from the same production batch for equal length and tension characteristics.
What if I only have the old belt? Send the marking printed on the belt — section, length and brand — and we will identify the exact replacement.
Do you handle timing belts? Yes. HTD, RPP, AT and classical timing belts with pitch, length, width and tooth profile verification.
Can you source pulleys? Yes. V-belt pulleys, timing pulleys, taper-lock bushings and QD bushings are available through our channels.
How quickly can you deliver? Standard V-belts and timing belts in stock sizes ship same-day from authorised distributors. Non-standard lengths may take 3–5 working days.
Why do matched sets matter? In multi-belt drives, each belt must share the load equally. If belts have different lengths (even 2–3mm difference), the shorter belt takes more load, wears faster, and fails earlier — causing cascading failures across the set. Matched sets ensure all belts have identical effective length from the same manufacturing batch.
