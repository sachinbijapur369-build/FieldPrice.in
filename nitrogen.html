PSA vs Membrane Comparison

Which nitrogen generation technology is right for your application?

PSA and membrane are the two main on-site nitrogen generation technologies. Each has distinct advantages depending on purity, flow rate, maintenance preference and budget.

FactorPSA (Pressure Swing Adsorption)Membrane
Purity Range95% to 99.999%95% to 99.5%
Flow Rate Range1 to 5000+ Nm³/hr1 to 2000+ Nm³/hr
Energy EfficiencyBetter at high purity (>99%)Better at low purity (<99%)
MaintenanceValve kit replacement every 3–5 years, CMS replacement every 10+ yearsMinimal — membrane modules are long-life
Capital CostHigher for low purity, competitive at high purityLower for low purity applications
Startup Time5–15 minutes to reach target purityInstant — nitrogen available immediately
Best ForPharma, electronics, food packaging, laser cutting (>99%)Blanketing, inerting, tyre inflation, fire suppression (<99%)

Technology selection tip

If your application needs purity above 99.5%, PSA is almost always the better choice. Below 99%, membrane may offer lower cost and simpler operation. Between 99–99.5%, both technologies compete — get quotes for both and compare the total cost of ownership including energy, maintenance and spares.

Buyer's Checklist

Know these six things before procuring a nitrogen generator.

Getting the purity, flow and scope right upfront prevents costly over-specification and ensures the system meets your actual production requirement.

  • Actual purity needed — Check your process specification. Over-specifying purity (e.g. 99.999% when 99% suffices) can double the system cost and energy consumption.
  • Peak and average flow rate — Measure actual nitrogen consumption during peak and normal production. Size the generator for peak demand with a nitrogen buffer tank.
  • Output pressure — Confirm required delivery pressure at point of use. Higher pressure may need a booster compressor — check if it is included.
  • Feed air system — Verify whether the quote includes the feed air compressor, refrigerant dryer, and pre-filtration system — or assumes existing infrastructure.
  • Installation scope — Piping, electrical, foundation, nitrogen storage tank, oxygen analyser, and commissioning — confirm what is included vs extra.
  • Operating cost analysis — Compare total cost (energy + maintenance + spares) over 5–10 years against cylinder/bulk liquid supply. On-site generation pays back faster at higher volumes.
