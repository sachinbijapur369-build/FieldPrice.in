Pro tip

Always verify the viscosity grade against your machine OEM manual. Using a higher or lower grade than specified can cause overheating, excessive wear, or insufficient film thickness — all of which lead to premature equipment failure.

Buyer's Checklist

Know these before procuring industrial lubricants and sealants.

The right information upfront leads to faster quotes, fewer specification errors and lower total cost. Check these before sending your requirement.

  • Viscosity grade — Know the ISO VG or SAE grade from your machine OEM manual or existing product label.
  • Base oil type — Mineral, synthetic (PAO/PAG/ester) or semi-synthetic. This determines performance and price tier.
  • OEM approval — Some machines require specific approvals (e.g. DIN 51524-2, Denison HF-0, AGMA 9005). Check your manual.
  • Operating temperature — Extreme heat or cold requires oils with specific pour point and flash point properties.
  • Compatibility — If switching brands, confirm the new oil is compatible with existing lubricant and seal materials.
  • Pack size & quantity — Litre, 20L bucket, 26L pail, 209L drum, or 1000L IBC. Procurement pricing varies significantly by pack size.
  • Sealant application — For sealants, specify the fluid being sealed, pressure, temperature, pipe material and thread standard.

What's the difference between mineral and synthetic oil? Mineral oils are refined from crude petroleum and cost less. Synthetic oils (PAO, PAG, ester) are chemically engineered for higher performance — better temperature stability, longer drain intervals and superior film strength. Cost is typically 2–4x more, but total cost of ownership can be lower in demanding applications.
How do I check OEM approvals? Your machine manual will list required lubricant specifications. We cross-reference against manufacturer approval sheets (e.g. DIN, AGMA, Denison, Cincinnati) before quoting to ensure the product meets your equipment's requirements.
