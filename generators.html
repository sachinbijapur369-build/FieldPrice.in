Sizing tip

Always account for motor starting currents (3–6× running load) when sizing generators. A generator that handles running load but cannot handle starting surges will trip on overload. Ask your supplier for a load study before finalising capacity.

Prime vs Standby Rating

Understanding generator ratings — the most common buyer mistake.

Confusing prime and standby ratings leads to either oversized purchases or overloaded generators. This table clarifies the difference.

Rating TypeDefinitionTypical Load FactorUse Case
Standby (ESP)Maximum power for emergency backup — grid is primary source70–80% of rated kVABackup for offices, hospitals, IT, residential
Prime (PRP)Continuous power where grid is unavailable or unreliable70–100% of rated kVARemote sites, mining, construction, islands
Continuous (COP)Base load power at constant load for unlimited hours70–100% at constant loadPower plants, co-generation, utility peaking

Key rule

A 125 kVA standby-rated generator should not be loaded beyond 100 kVA continuously. If your application requires continuous prime power, ensure the generator is prime-rated — not standby-rated being used as prime. This is the most common specification error in generator purchases.

Buyer's Checklist

Know these six things before buying a generator.

Getting the specification right upfront leads to faster quotes and avoids costly oversizing or dangerous undersizing. Check these before sending your requirement.

  • Connected load (kW/kVA) — Calculate total connected load including motor starting currents. Factor in future expansion if planned.
  • Prime or standby — Backup for grid failures (standby) or primary power source (prime). Rating type affects capacity and price.
  • Fuel type — Diesel is standard for most industrial applications. Gas is preferred where natural gas supply is reliable and emissions matter.
  • Acoustic requirement — Silent/enclosed sets for urban and residential areas. Open sets acceptable for industrial zones. Check local noise regulations.
  • Installation scope — Civil foundation, exhaust piping, fuel tank, electrical cabling, AMF panel, earthing and commissioning — confirm what is included.
  • Service & warranty — Authorised service at your location, spare parts lead time, warranty period and AMC terms. Critical for uptime.
